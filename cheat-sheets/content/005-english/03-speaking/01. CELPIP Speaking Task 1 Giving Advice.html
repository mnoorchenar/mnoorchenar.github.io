CELPIP Speaking Task 1: Giving Advice

1 Moving, Housing, and New Places

Topics: moving to a new city or country, choosing an apartment, adjusting to a new neighbourhood, living with roommates, buying a home.

General Template

Well, if I were you, I would [main advice]. First of all, [advice 1 and a short reason].

Another thing you could do is [advice 2]. On top of that, [advice 3 or a quick example].

However, [one warning or a tip to stay realistic].

I’m sure that [positive result]. Good luck, and let me know how it goes.

1.1 Settling Into a New City

Your friend is moving to a new city for work and does not know anyone there. Give advice on how to settle in and make friends.

Sample

Well, if I were you, I would focus on joining groups in the first month. First of all, find a hobby club or a sports team, because it’s the fastest way to meet like-minded people. Also, say yes to invitations from your coworkers, even if you feel tired. Small social gatherings like lunch or after-work coffee can turn into real friendships.

Another thing you could do is explore your neighbourhood on foot. Visit the local cafés and parks, and you’ll soon start to recognize familiar faces. On top of that, you could volunteer on weekends, which is a great way to build a support network.

However, be patient with yourself. It takes time to settle in, so don’t get discouraged if it feels lonely at first.

I’m sure that in a few months you’ll feel right at home. Good luck, and let me know how it goes.

Vocabulary

Word Meaning Example sentence
meet like-minded people meet others with similar interests A book club is a good place to meet like-minded people.
social gatherings informal events where people meet I try to attend social gatherings to make new friends.
neighbourhood the area around where you live Our neighbourhood has a lovely park.
build a support network create a group of people who help you It took her a year to build a support network in Canada.
settle in become comfortable in a new place It took me a few weeks to settle in.
volunteer work without pay to help others I volunteer at the food bank on Saturdays.

1.2 Meeting Neighbours ⭐

Your friend recently moved into a new neighbourhood and does not know anyone there. Give advice on how to meet the neighbours and feel at home.

Recent exam, October 2026: reported in the Oct 2 compiled list.

Sample

Well, if I were you, I would start with a simple greeting. First of all, say hello whenever you see someone in the hallway or on the street, because a friendly smile breaks the ice. Also, you could drop by the local café or library, since the regulars there are usually happy to chat.

Another thing you could do is join a community event, like a street sale or a park cleanup. On top of that, you could bake something and reach out to your neighbours with a small gesture of welcome.

However, don’t expect instant friendships. Just stay patient and keep a positive attitude.

I’m sure that you’ll feel part of the neighbourhood soon. Good luck, and let me know how it goes.

Vocabulary

Word Meaning Example sentence
break the ice make people feel more relaxed at a first meeting A joke can break the ice at a party.

reach out to contact someone to offer help or friendship She reached out to her new neighbours.
community event a public activity for local residents The community event drew hundreds of people.
gesture an action that shows a feeling Bringing flowers was a kind gesture.
positive attitude a hopeful way of thinking A positive attitude helps in a new city.

1.3 Downtown vs Suburbs

Your cousin is looking for an apartment. Advise whether to rent near downtown or in the suburbs.

Sample

Well, in my opinion, it depends on your daily routine. First of all, if you work downtown, I’d rent near the city centre. You’ll save time and money on your commute, and you’ll have restaurants and shops within walking distance.

On the other hand, the suburbs offer more space for a lower price. If you want a quiet street and a bigger place, that could be a better value for money. Just remember that you’ll probably need a car or a long bus ride.

Another thing you could do is visit both areas at different times of the day. Check the noise, the traffic, and the amenities nearby, like groceries and parks. Also, set a clear rental budget before you start, so you don’t fall in love with a place you can’t afford.

If I were you, I’d choose the area that matches my lifestyle, not just the lowest rent. I’m sure you’ll find something great.

Vocabulary

Word Meaning Example sentence
commute the daily trip between home and work My commute takes forty minutes by train.
value for money good quality for the price paid This apartment is excellent value for money.
amenities useful facilities near a home The building has amenities like a gym and a laundry room.
rental budget the amount you can spend on rent Our rental budget is 1,500 dollars a month.
within walking distance close enough to reach on foot The subway is within walking distance.
suburbs residential areas outside the city centre They moved to the suburbs for a bigger yard.

1.4 Moving Abroad

Your friend is moving abroad for the first time. Give advice on how to prepare.

Sample

Well, if I were you, I would start preparing at least two months in advance. First of all, check your visa and all your documents, and make copies of everything. Losing a passport abroad is a real nightmare.

Next, learn about the cost of living in your new city, so you can make a realistic budget. Another thing you could do is research housing early and book a short-term place for the first few weeks. That way, you can look for a permanent apartment without pressure.

On top of that, learn a few basic phrases in the local language and read about the local customs. It helps you avoid culture shock, or at least makes it easier to adapt.

However, don’t try to plan every detail. Leave some room for surprises. I’m sure that it will be an amazing adventure. Good luck, and let me know how it goes.

Vocabulary

Word Meaning Example sentence
visa official permission to enter or live in a country She applied for a student visa.
cost of living the money needed for daily expenses The cost of living is high in big cities.
culture shock confusion felt in an unfamiliar culture He felt culture shock during his first week abroad.
short-term lasting only a brief time We booked a short-term rental for the first month.
local customs traditional habits of a place It’s polite to learn the local customs before you travel.

1.6 Living With Roommates ⭐

Your coworker is about to live with roommates for the first time. Give advice for a good living arrangement.

Recent exam, October 2026: reported in the Oct 2 compiled list (a niece sharing accommodation with several roommates) and on Oct 11 (a friend in a dormitory).

Sample

Well, if I were in your position, I would talk openly with my roommates before moving in. First of all, agree on the basic house rules, like quiet hours, guests, and cleaning. It’s much easier to set expectations at the start than to fix problems later.

Another thing you could do is make a simple chore chart and a plan for splitting the bills. For example, you can use an app to share costs for rent, internet, and groceries, so nobody feels treated unfairly.

Also, respect each other’s personal space. Even good friends need time alone. On top of that, if something bothers you, speak up politely right away instead of letting resentment build up.

However, don’t expect to become best friends with everyone. A friendly, considerate relationship is enough. I’m sure that it will work out well. Good luck!

Vocabulary

Word Meaning Example sentence
house rules agreed guidelines for shared living Our house rules include no loud music after 10 p.m.
splitting the bills sharing the cost of expenses Splitting the bills equally keeps things simple.
personal space privacy and room to be alone Everyone needs some personal space at home.
resentment bitter feelings about unfair treatment Resentment grows when chores are not shared.
considerate thoughtful about other people’s feelings He is a considerate roommate who always cleans up.
chore chart a schedule showing who does which household task We hung a chore chart on the fridge.

1.8 Buying a House

Your friend wants to buy a house but is unsure about the cost. Give advice on what to consider.

Sample

Well, if I were you, I would look at the full picture before deciding. First of all, figure out how much you can afford. Don’t think only about the down payment. Consider your monthly mortgage payments, property taxes, insurance, and repairs.

Another thing you could do is talk to a bank or a mortgage broker to get pre-approved. That way, you’ll know your real budget and avoid wasting time on houses that are too expensive.

On top of that, keep an emergency fund. For example, a broken furnace or a leaking roof can cost thousands of dollars, and as an owner, it’s your responsibility.

On the other hand, renting for another year or two might be smart if your income isn’t stable yet. There’s no need to rush.

I’m sure that you’ll make the right choice if you take your time. Good luck, and let me know how it goes.

Vocabulary

Word Meaning Example sentence
down payment the first lump sum paid when buying a home They saved for five years for the down payment.
mortgage a loan used to buy property Our mortgage is paid monthly.
mortgage broker a person who helps you find a home loan A mortgage broker compared several rates for us.
pre-approved officially cleared in advance for a loan We got pre-approved before visiting open houses.
stable steady and not likely to change She wants a stable job before buying a house.
emergency fund savings kept for unexpected costs An emergency fund helped when the car broke down.

Well, if I were you, I would be polite and reliable from the very first day. First of all, always pay the rent on time, because that’s what landlords care about most. Set up an automatic payment so you never forget.

However, don’t be afraid to stand up for your rights if something isn’t fixed after a reasonable time.

I’m sure that you’ll have a smooth rental experience. Good luck, and let me know how it goes.

2.1 Choosing Between Job Offers

Your friend has two job offers, one with higher pay and one with better work-life balance. Give advice on which to choose.

Sample

Well, if I were you, I would think carefully about what matters most right now. First of all, ask yourself whether the extra money is really necessary. If you have financial obligations, like rent or a loan, the higher salary might be the safer choice.

On the other hand, a good work-life balance protects your health and your relationships. Money can’t buy back the time you lose to stress and long hours.

Another thing you could do is talk to current employees at both companies. Ask about the workload, the team, and the chances of career growth. Also, write a list of pros and cons, which makes the decision much clearer.

Personally, I’d lean toward the balanced job if the pay is enough to live comfortably. You can always negotiate a raise later.

I’m sure that you’ll make a wise choice. Good luck, and let me know how it goes.

Vocabulary

Word Meaning Example sentence
financial obligations money you must pay regularly He has financial obligations like a mortgage.
work-life balance a healthy split between work and personal time This company values work-life balance.

career growth chances to advance in your job The firm offers great career growth.
negotiate discuss to reach an agreement She negotiated a higher salary.
lean toward tend to prefer I lean toward the second offer.

2.2 Interview Tomorrow ⭐

Your friend has a job interview tomorrow and is nervous. Give advice on how to prepare.

Recent exam, October 2026: reported on Oct 4 (several reports, including a first-time interview).

Sample

Well, if I were you, I would stay calm and use tonight wisely. First of all, research the company. Read its website and understand its products and values, so you can show real interest.

Another thing you could do is practise answering common questions out loud, like “Tell me about yourself” or “Why should we hire you?” Prepare two or three examples of your achievements, and use specific numbers if you can. Also, prepare a couple of questions to ask the interviewer, because it shows you’re enthusiastic.

On top of that, lay out your clothes and plan your route, so you arrive ten minutes early. Then get a good night’s sleep.

However, don’t worry about being perfect. Interviewers just want to see a confident, honest person. Take a deep breath and smile.

I’m sure that you’ll do great. Good luck, and let me know how it goes.

Vocabulary

Word Meaning Example sentence
achievements things you have successfully done List your achievements on your résumé.
interviewer the person who asks questions in an interview The interviewer was very friendly.
enthusiastic showing strong interest She was enthusiastic about the role.

2.3 Quitting to Start a Business

Your colleague is thinking of quitting a stable job to start a business. Give advice.

Sample

Well, if I were in your position, I would not rush into it. First of all, test your idea before you quit. Start the business part-time on evenings and weekends, so you can see if there is real demand.

Another thing you could do is build a financial cushion. Most new businesses take a long time to make a profit, so I’d save at least six months of living expenses. Also, write a clear business plan with your costs, customers, and goals.

On top of that, talk to people who have already done it. They can warn you about mistakes and give you useful contacts.

On the other hand, if you’ve prepared well, taking a calculated risk can be rewarding. Many people regret not trying more than failing.

I’m sure that with good planning you can succeed. Good luck, and let me know how it goes.

Vocabulary

Word Meaning Example sentence
demand the desire of customers to buy something There is strong demand for healthy snacks.
financial cushion savings that protect you in hard times A financial cushion reduces stress.
business plan a document describing goals and finances The bank asked for my business plan.
calculated risk a risk taken after careful thought Opening a shop was a calculated risk.
make a profit earn more than you spend The café made a profit in its second year.

2.4 Overwork and Exhaustion

Your friend works too many hours and is exhausted. Give advice on how to manage the workload.

Sample

Well, if I were you, I would make some changes before burning out. First of all, list all your tasks and decide which ones are truly urgent. Learn to prioritize, so you focus on what really matters.

Another thing you could do is talk to your manager. Explain that your workload is too heavy and suggest solutions, like delegating some tasks or adjusting deadlines. Most bosses would rather solve a problem early than lose a tired employee.

Also, set clear boundaries. For example, turn off work emails after 7 p.m. and take a real lunch break. On top of that, make time for exercise and sleep, because rest actually improves your productivity.

However, be patient. Changing habits takes a few weeks.

I’m sure that you’ll feel much better soon. Good luck, and let me know how it goes.

Vocabulary

Word Meaning Example sentence

workload the amount of work to do A heavy workload can cause stress.
delegating give a task to someone else Good managers delegate tasks.
boundaries limits you set to protect your time Set boundaries between work and home.
productivity how much work you get done Rest improves productivity.

Well, if I were you, I would focus on being friendly and prepared. First of all, arrive a little early on the first day and dress appropriately. Being on time shows that you’re reliable.

Another thing you could do is introduce yourself to everyone with a smile and learn people’s names. Also, listen more than you talk during the first weeks. Take notes, ask questions, and show that you’re eager to learn.

On top of that, be proactive. If you finish a task, ask what else you can help with. Small initiatives like that get noticed quickly.

However, don’t try to impress everyone at once or pretend to know everything. Being honest about what you don’t know builds trust.

I’m sure that you’ll fit in well. Good luck, and let me know how it goes.

Vocabulary

Word Meaning Example sentence
reliable able to be trusted She is a reliable team member.
proactive taking action before being asked A proactive employee solves problems early.
initiatives the ability to start things on your own He showed great initiative.
fit in feel part of a group I quickly fit in with my new coworkers.

2.6 Presentation Nerves ⭐

Your coworker is nervous about giving a presentation to the whole team. Give advice on how to prepare and stay calm.

Recent exam, October 2026: reported in the Oct 2 compiled list (a first presentation at work).

Sample

Well, if I were in your position, I would prepare early and practise a lot. First of all, organize your ideas into three clear points, with a short introduction and a strong ending. A simple structure is easier to remember.

Another thing you could do is rehearse in front of a friend or a mirror, and time yourself. Practice builds confidence, and you’ll be less likely to freeze on the day.

Also, use short slides with pictures, not long paragraphs. On top of that, take slow, deep breaths before you start, and speak at a steady pace. If you feel nervous, remember that the audience wants you to succeed.

However, don’t memorize every word. If you forget something, just keep going calmly. Nobody will notice.

I’m sure that you’ll deliver a great presentation. Good luck, and let me know how it goes.

Vocabulary

Word Meaning Example sentence
confidence belief in your own ability Practice gives you confidence.
rehearse practise before the real event We rehearsed the speech twice.
steady pace a calm, even speed Speak at a steady pace.
audience the people listening The audience applauded warmly.

I’m sure that you’ll do great. Good luck, and let me know how it goes.
